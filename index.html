THE INSTITUTIONS BEHIND THE NUMBERS

Local institutions.
National significance.

State courts are where everyday life meets the justice system.

A housing dispute. A family matter. A traffic citation. A criminal proceeding. A state-court caseload is a record of institutional demand, but each entry also represents a person, a business, or a community seeking a resolution.

CourtAtlas makes research easier to explore. Read the national context, examine changes in reported workloads, and test a transparent planning scenario. Every analysis tells you its period, source, and limits.

How to read the research ↗
TRIAL COURTS

Where cases begin.

Trial courts hear evidence and resolve matters within their jurisdiction. General and limited-jurisdiction courts can have very different mixes of work. Compare like structures before comparing totals.

APPELLATE COURTS

Where decisions are reviewed.

Appeals follow a different workflow from trial cases. Incoming appeals, decisions, and pending matters need their own definitions and should be analyzed separately.

THE DATA SCIENCE LENS

Volume is only the beginning.

A filing count does not capture complexity, time, or service needs. Pair workload measures with case types, reporting coverage, and clear denominators to build a more useful picture.

UNDERSTANDING THE WORK

One justice system.
Many kinds of cases.

State-court research becomes more meaningful when the categories have a human context.

Civil matters+

Civil courts address disputes between people and organizations. Contract claims, small claims, torts, and real-property matters can produce different volumes and processing demands. A category may be counted differently across court structures; check the source definition.

Criminal proceedings+

Criminal caseloads concern alleged violations of criminal law. Cases, charges, and appearances are different units. A case with several charges should not silently become several cases in an analysis, and a disposition should not be mistaken for a conviction.

Domestic relations+

Family-related matters can involve divorce, support, and parenting issues. Court structure and reporting practices affect their classification. Service access and timely resolution matter, but public aggregate reporting is different from access to private family records.

Juvenile matters+

Juvenile data can cover distinct proceedings, including delinquency and dependency. Definitions and confidentiality protections vary. This website uses aggregate research and does not display identifiable youth records.

Traffic and local matters+

Traffic and ordinance matters often sit within limited-jurisdiction courts. Large volumes may coexist with very different processing requirements from complex civil litigation. A filing count measures demand, not the intensity of judicial work.

THE RESEARCH CONTRACT

Beautiful data.
Honest interpretation.

Clarity is a design choice. So is transparency.

01

Know the scope.

National context and preliminary reporting cohorts are labeled separately. A chart based on a subset of jurisdictions is not presented as a complete US total.

02

Respect the definitions.

Incoming, outgoing, and pending cases describe different measures. Clearance is dispositions divided by incoming cases for a matched period and scope; it does not measure fairness or case quality.

03

Show the assumptions.

The planning studio is hypothetical arithmetic. It is not a forecast or a staffing recommendation. Published figures retain their approximate wording and source period.

04

Keep the source close.

Every research visualization links to its publisher. These are a curated research snapshot reviewed October 13, 2026, not a live connection to court records.
